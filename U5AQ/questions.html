1. A statistics website simulates taking 1,000 simple random samples of size 50 from a large population. For each sample, it calculates the sample mean. The dotplot of those 1,000 sample means is best described as:
            A. The population distribution
            B. The distribution of the original data values
            C. An approximate sampling distribution of the sample mean
            D. A confidence interval for the population mean
  2. A school wants to estimate the true proportion of students who use a practice website before tests. In one simple random sample of 200 students, 126 say they use the website. Which value is the point estimate?
            A. 200
            B. 126
            C. 0.63
            D. The true proportion of all students who use the website
  3. Which statements about an unbiased estimator are true?
            I. An estimator is unbiased if the center of its sampling distribution equals the true population parameter.
            II. An unbiased estimator will always equal the true parameter for every sample.
            III. Sample proportions are generally unbiased estimators of population proportions.
            IV. Larger sample sizes reduce bias.
    A. I and III only
    B. I, II, and III only
    C. II and IV only
    D. I, III, and IV only
  4. A population has mean 80 and standard deviation 12. Samples of size 36 are repeatedly selected. What are the mean and standard deviation of the sampling distribution of the sample mean?
            A. Mean = 80, standard deviation = 12
            B. Mean = 80, standard deviation = 2
            C. Mean = 13.33, standard deviation = 12
            D. Mean = 13.33, standard deviation = 2
  5. In a large population, 40% of students say they prefer MCQs over FRQs. A simple random sample of 100 students is selected. Which gives the mean and standard deviation of the sampling distribution of the sample proportion?
            A. Mean = 0.40, standard deviation = 0.049
            B. Mean = 0.40, standard deviation = 0.240
            C. Mean = 40, standard deviation = 4.9
            D. Mean = 100, standard deviation = 0.049
  6. A sample of 60 students is selected without replacement from a school of 400 students. A student wants to use the formula for the standard deviation of the sampling distribution of the sample proportion. What is the issue?
            A. The sample was selected without replacement, but the 10% condition is still satisfied.
            B. The sample was selected without replacement, and the 10% condition is not satisfied.
            C. The sample size is too small for any sampling distribution.
            D. The sample proportion can never be approximately normal.
  7. A poll takes a simple random sample of 80 students from a large school. Suppose the true proportion of students who like statistics is 0.70. Can the sampling distribution of the sample proportion be treated as approximately normal?
            A. Yes, because 80 is greater than 30.
            B. Yes, because expected successes = 56 and expected failures = 24.
            C. No, because the sample size is less than 100.
            D. No, because the true population proportion is unknown.
  8. A population distribution is strongly skewed right with mean 25 and standard deviation 10. A simple random sample of size 45 is selected. Which statement is best?
            A. The sample mean will be exactly 25.
            B. The sampling distribution of the sample mean is approximately normal because the sample size is large enough for the Central Limit Theorem.
            C. The sampling distribution of the sample mean is strongly skewed right because the population is strongly skewed right.
            D. The standard deviation of the sampling distribution is 10.
  9. A city estimates the proportion of residents who support a new bus route. A 95% confidence interval is 0.52 to 0.60. Which interpretation is correct?
            A. There is a 95% probability that the true proportion of residents who support the bus route is between 0.52 and 0.60.
            B. 95% of residents have support levels between 0.52 and 0.60.
            C. We are 95% confident that the interval from 0.52 to 0.60 captures the true proportion of residents who support the bus route.
            D. If we take many samples, exactly 95% of sample proportions will be between 0.52 and 0.60.
  10. A 90% confidence interval for a population proportion is 0.34 to 0.46. Which statement is true?
            I. The point estimate is 0.40.
            II. The margin of error is 0.06.
            III. A 95% confidence interval from the same data would be narrower.
            IV. The interval gives plausible values for the population proportion.
    A. I and II only
    B. I, II, and IV only
    C. II and III only
    D. I, II, III, and IV
  11. A researcher wants to estimate the proportion of students who complete the AP Stats review packet. She wants a margin of error no larger than 0.04 at 95% confidence. She uses a conservative estimate of 0.50 for the population proportion. Which sample size is closest?
            A. 385
            B. 601
            C. 1,201
            D. 2,401
  12. A random sample of 25 students has a mean quiz score of 82 and a sample standard deviation of 10. The teacher wants to construct a 95% confidence interval for the true mean quiz score of all students taught with this review method. Which critical value should be used?
            A. z* = 1.96
            B. z* = 1.645
            C. t* with 24 degrees of freedom
            D. t* with 25 degrees of freedom
  13. A random sample of 100 students gives a 95% confidence interval of 6.8 to 8.2 hours for the true mean number of hours students sleep on a school night. Which statements are true?
            I. The point estimate is 7.5 hours.
            II. The margin of error is 0.7 hours.
            III. The interval estimates a population mean, not a sample mean.
            IV. The interval proves that every student sleeps between 6.8 and 8.2 hours.
    A. I and II only
    B. I, II, and III only
    C. II and IV only
    D. I, II, III, and IV
  14. A 95% confidence interval for the proportion of students who prefer digital flashcards is 0.41 to 0.57. Which change would definitely decrease the margin of error, assuming everything else stays the same?
            A. Use a smaller sample size.
            B. Use a 99% confidence level.
            C. Use an 80% confidence level.
            D. Use a more biased sampling method.
  15. A survey of 500 randomly selected students finds that 58% support adding more AP Statistics practice problems to the school website. The school has about 8,000 students. Which conditions are satisfied for constructing a one-sample confidence interval for a proportion?
            I. Random: satisfied because the students were randomly selected.
            II. 10% condition: satisfied because 500 is less than 10% of 8,000.
            III. Large Counts: satisfied because there are 290 successes and 210 failures.
            IV. Normality: not satisfied because the population distribution is not stated to be normal.
    A. I and II only
    B. I, II, and III only
    C. II and IV only
    D. I, II, III, and IV
