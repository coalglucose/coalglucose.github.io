1) A school wants to estimate the proportion of students who support starting school 30 minutes later. The principal plans to survey the first 100 students who enter the cafeteria on Monday morning.

(a) Explain why the principal’s plan might not produce a representative sample of students at the school.

The principal’s plan might not produce a representative sample because the first 100 students who enter the cafeteria on Monday morning may differ from the rest of the students at the school. For example, students who arrive early, eat breakfast at school, or go to the cafeteria before class may have different opinions about starting school later than students who do not enter the cafeteria that morning. This sampling method could lead to undercoverage of students who do not use the cafeteria and could introduce bias.

(b) Describe a better method for selecting 100 students to survey.

        A better method would be to select a simple random sample of 100 students from a complete list of all students at the school. Number every student on the school roster from 1 to N, where N is the total number of students at the school. Use the same number of digits for each label. For example, if there are 2,000 students, label the students 0001 to 2000. Then, using a random digits table, read from left to right in groups of the correct number of digits. Ignore any numbers that are not assigned to a student, such as 0000 or numbers greater than N, and ignore repeated numbers. Continue until 100 different student numbers have been selected. The students whose numbers are selected would be surveyed.

(c) The principal wants to make sure freshmen, sophomores, juniors, and seniors are all represented in the sample. Describe a sampling method that addresses this goal.

The principal could use a stratified random sample with grade level as the strata. First, separate all students into four groups: freshmen, sophomores, juniors, and seniors. Then randomly select students from each grade level. For example, the principal could randomly select 25 students from each grade level to create a sample of 100 students total. This would make sure that all four grade levels are represented in the sample.

(d) Suppose 68 of the 100 surveyed students support starting school later. Explain whether it would be reasonable to generalize this result to all students at the school.

 It would not be reasonable to generalize the result to all students at the school if the 100 students were selected using the principal’s original plan of surveying the first 100 students who enter the cafeteria. That method is not random and may not represent the entire student body. Even though 68 out of the 100 surveyed students support starting school later, the sample may be biased because it only includes students who entered the cafeteria on Monday morning. Therefore, the result should not be generalized to all students at the school.

2) A company wants to test whether a new type of running shoe improves mile times. It recruits 80 runners. Each runner’s usual mile time is recorded before the study begins.

(a) Describe how the company could design an experiment to compare the new shoe with the current shoe.

The experimental units are the 80 runners. The explanatory variable is the type of running shoe used. The treatments are the new running shoe and the current running shoe.

The response variable is the improvement in mile time, calculated as:

improvement = usual mile time - mile time after using the assigned shoe

The company should randomly assign the 80 runners to two treatment groups. Forty runners should be assigned to use the new running shoe, and the remaining 40 runners should be assigned to use the current shoe. This will be performed by numbering the 80 runners from 01 to 80. Using a random digits table, read from left to right in two-digit groups. Ignore 00, any number greater than 80, and any repeated number. Continue until 40 different runner numbers have been selected. Assign the 40 selected runners to use the new running shoe. Assign the remaining 40 runners to use the current running shoe. This random assignment gives each runner an equal chance of being assigned to either shoe group and helps create two groups that are similar before the treatments are applied.

All runners should follow the same procedure as much as possible. For example, each runner should use the assigned shoe for the same amount of time before the final mile trial. All runners should run one mile on the same type of course or track, with the same timing method, and under similar conditions. The company should record each runner’s mile time after using the assigned shoe. Then, for each runner, calculate:

improvement = usual mile time - final mile time

Then compare the mean improvement for the runners assigned to the new shoe with the mean improvement for the runners assigned to the current shoe.

(b) The company finds that runners using the new shoe had significantly faster mile times. Explain whether the company can conclude that the new shoe caused the improvement.

        Yes, the company can conclude that the new shoe caused the improvement for the runners in the experiment, as long as the runners were randomly assigned to the two shoe groups and the groups were treated the same in every way except for the type of shoe used.

This was an experiment because the company imposed a treatment on the runners: either the new running shoe or the current running shoe. Random assignment helps create two groups that are similar before the treatments are applied. Since the new-shoe group had significantly faster mile times, the difference is unlikely to be due to chance alone and can reasonably be attributed to the shoe type.

Therefore, there is evidence that the new running shoe caused faster mile times for the runners in the experiment.

(c) Explain whether the company can generalize the results to all runners.

No, the company cannot necessarily generalize the results to all runners.

The 80 runners were recruited, not randomly selected from the population of all runners. Because the runners were not randomly selected, they may not be representative of all runners. For example, the recruited runners may be more experienced, more competitive, healthier, or more motivated than the general population of runners.

The results can only be generalized to runners similar to the 80 runners who participated in the experiment. To generalize to all runners, the company would need to randomly select runners from the population of all runners.
