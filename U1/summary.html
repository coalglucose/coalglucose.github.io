To describe a distribution, use SOCS with non-absolute language

  • Shape (approximately normal? skew?)
  • Outlier (Any points seem “outside” the usual range?)

  • Spread (Describe range, or use IQR or SD if given and appropriate)

Resistant statistics

  • Mean and standard deviation are non-resistant to outliers
  • Median and IQR are resistant to outliers

Skew

  • If a graph is “skewed right” the extreme values lie on the right side.
  • If a graph is “skewed left” the extreme values lie on the left side.

Comparing Distributions

  • Use SOCS

Meaning of standard deviation in context

  • On average, the number of [variable in context] is about [standard deviation in context] away from the mean value of [mean]

Modifying Datasets

  • Transforming each value x to ax+b changes the standard deviation to a*σ and the mean to a*μ+b

Displaying data

  • Bar Charts and Pie Charts for categorical data
  • Dotplots, stemplots, histograms, box-and-whisker plots, and cumulative frequency plots for quantitative data
