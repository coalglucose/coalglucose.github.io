Unit 5D: Chi-Squared & Lin-Reg

Chi-squared (χ2) tests are hypothesis tests that verify whether a distribution of frequencies matches another. This is often done with a calculator. A chi-squared distribution is always right-skewed and the test is always a right-tailed test.

CHI-SQUARED GOODNESS-OF-FIT TEST

The χ2 GOF test is used to determine whether the distribution of one categorical variable in a population matches a claimed or expected distribution.

> For example, you might test whether the colors of M&M's follow the manufacturer's advertised proportions or whether births are equally likely on each day of the week.

The hypotheses compare the entire population distribution to the claimed distribution. One way to tell this from the other tests is that there is only one distribution that was sampled, and you are comparing it to a distribution that they predict.

> H0: The population follows the stated distribution.

>HA: The population does not follow the stated distribution.

Assumptions:

  • Random sample
  • Independence
  • 10% condition
  • We have a large enough sample
  • Each expected frequency is greater than 5
  • Calculate using E = n*p for each box, or use calculator

degrees of freedom (d.f.) = k - 1, where k = number of categories

Test statistic:

Your chi-squared critical value and probability will be given by the calculator. Remember to draw a graph with the critical region shaded and the chi-squared cv and test statistic labeled.

CHI-SQUARED TEST FOR INDEPENDENCE & CHI-SQUARED TEST FOR HOMOGENEITY

These tests both have the same underlying math principles and both involve two distributions from two samples. The only difference between them is the phrasing of the question that’s asked.

> If the problem asks if two variables are related at all or the distributions are independent, use a test for independence.

> If the problem asks if two distributions are the same for two samples, use a test for homogeneity.
