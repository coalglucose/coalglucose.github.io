Two-Variable Data

General Rules:

  1. Use let statements, define your variables carefully

Topics for this Unit:

Scatterplots

  • Bivariate data involves a response (dependent) variable and an explanatory (independent) variable.
  • Segmented bar graphs and mosaic plots display relative frequencies (percents) of categorical variables
  • When describing a scatter plot, talk about strength, direction, form, and unusual features
  • Points of high leverage are points with x-values that are far away from the mean.
  • Influential points are points that, if removed, would modify the regression line significantly.

LSRL

  • Include let statements
  • ŷ = a + bx
  • r is the correlation coefficient. It is unitless and measures strength of linear association
  • b = r * (sy/sx)
  • r2 is the coefficient of determination. It measures the percent of variation in y that is determined by the linear relationship between y and x.
  • Residual = y - ŷ
  • Slope interpretation: On average, for each unit increase in [x], [predicted y] increases by [slope].
  • Y-intercept interpretation: When the [x value is 0], we predict that the [y value is __].
  • Extrapolation often leads to inaccurate predictions
